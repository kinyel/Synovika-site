"Where do I buy a website?" sounds like a simple question. In practice there are three quite different ways to get one, and the right choice depends on your time, your skills and how much control you want.

## Option 1: a ready-made template

You buy a design template, often for a popular platform, then set it up, write the content and host it yourself (or pay someone to).

**Good for:** people comfortable with technology, on a tight budget, who want something standard.

**Watch out for:**
- the time it takes to set up and maintain;
- updates and security patches, which are your job;
- templates that look good in a preview but load slowly on phones.

## Option 2: a website builder

You pay a monthly fee to an online service, choose a design, and build the site in your browser with drag-and-drop tools.

**Good for:** getting online quickly without technical help.

**Watch out for:**
- you rent rather than own: stop paying and the site goes;
- moving to another provider later can be hard;
- monthly fees add up over the years, often in foreign currency;
- limited control over speed and how the site appears in search.

## Option 3: a site built for you

A developer or company designs and builds the site around your business.

**Good for:** businesses that want a site that fits them, works well on ordinary phones, and grows with them.

**Watch out for:**
- the build costs more up front;
- some built sites need the developer for every change. Ask for an **admin panel**, so your team can update the content itself;
- make sure you get the logins to everything, and the domain in your name.

## Side by side

| | Template | Website builder | Built for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up-front cost | Low | Low | Higher |
| Ongoing cost | Hosting and upkeep | Monthly fee | Hosting and support |
| Your time | A lot | Some | Little |
| Fits your business | Partly | Partly | Closely |
| You own it | Yes | Not really | Yes, if agreed |
| Everyday changes | You, if you can | You | You, with an admin panel |

## Whichever you choose

- **Keep the domain in your company's name.**
- **Test it on an ordinary Android phone, on mobile data,** before you pay the final bill.
- **Ask what changes cost** after launch.
- **Check the basics of the law:** a contact form collects personal data, so you need a privacy notice. See [NDPA basics](/insights/ndpa-basics-small-business-websites/).
