The gate is where an estate's security happens, and in many Nigerian estates it still runs on a paper logbook and a phone. It works, but it's slow, it depends on residents answering calls, and the record it leaves is hard to search. An estate visitor management system is the software alternative. Here's how one works, and how to choose.

## How a visitor management system works

Most systems follow the same pattern:

1. **The resident expects a visitor** and creates a code, usually on their phone, valid for a set time.
2. **The visitor gets the code** by message.
3. **At the gate, the guard checks the code.** If it's valid for that house and that time, the visitor goes in.
4. **The entry is recorded,** with the time and the house, and the resident may be told their visitor has arrived.

Instead of the guard asking "who are you here to see?" and phoning the house, the resident has already said yes. That's the whole idea: the gate knows who's expected. We compare it in detail with paper in [visitor codes versus logbooks](/insights/estate-visitor-codes-vs-logbooks/).

## What each person needs from it

### Residents

- Creating a code in seconds, on any phone.
- Codes that expire, so they can't be reused or shared forever.
- Ways to handle regular visitors, domestic staff and deliveries.

### Guards

- A simple yes or no, on a clear screen, fast enough to beat the logbook.
- Something that still works when the network is weak.
- Training that takes minutes, not days.

### Estate managers and committees

- A searchable record of entries.
- Control over who can see that record.
- Simple administration when residents move in or out.

### Visitors

- Nothing to install. A plain message with a code is enough.

## Questions to ask before your estate chooses

1. **What happens when the network or power goes down?** There must be a fallback, decided in advance.
2. **Who can see the entry records?** A record of who visits whom is personal data, and the Nigeria Data Protection Act applies to it. Decide who sees it and how long it's kept.
3. **How does it handle regular visitors and staff?** Nannies, drivers, cleaners and delivery riders come often; the system should make that easy.
4. **What does the guard actually do?** Ask for a demonstration at a real gate, with a real guard.
5. **What hardware is involved?** Some systems need only phones; others connect to barriers or turnstiles. Know what you're buying.
6. **What does it cost each year,** for the software, any hardware, and support?
7. **What happens to your data if you leave?** You should be able to take it with you.

## Making the change

Introduce it in stages: run the system alongside the logbook for a few weeks, train guards on every shift, and help residents set it up. The estates that get the most from these systems are the ones that plan the change, not just the purchase.
